A festive puzzle for a quiet break

This Christmas word search offers twelve familiar festive words, from baubles and tinsel to mince pies and carols. Print it for yourself, a shared afternoon or a small activity group. It is an original resource made for this webpage, not a page copied from our Cozy Christmas book.

The PDF is free to open without an account. The separate answer sheet means you can hand out the puzzle without giving away the solution. There is no timed challenge and no expectation that everyone celebrates Christmas in the same way.

Directions and word list

Find twelve words in a 15 × 15 grid. Read left to right, top to bottom or diagonally down to the right. There are no backwards words. Ignore spaces: MINCE PIES is hidden as MINCEPIES. Shared letters can belong to more than one word.

  • BAUBLES
  • TINSEL
  • CAROLS
  • MINCE PIES
  • CRACKERS
  • HOLLY
  • CANDLE
  • PUDDING
  • STOCKING
  • PRESENTS
  • WREATH
  • SNOWFLAKE

Print the puzzle, keep the answers aside

Use A4 portrait at actual size / 100%. Download the PDF and print it directly, rather than using a screenshot of a phone screen. Check the print preview and avoid extra browser headers. Black-and-white printing is sufficient. If anything clips, confirm A4 is selected; “fit” can help a mismatched printer but reduces the type size.

Grid letters are 20 point and the word list is 18 point at 100%, with white space around the grid and margins of at least 15 mm. Try a copy before printing for a group. The answer sheet contains the identical grid with highlighted word paths and row/column start and end coordinates. Count rows from the top and columns from the left.

Make a shared activity comfortable

Offer pencils and let people choose to solve alone or with someone else. If the full list feels too much, select three words to start with. You can give the first-letter location from the answer sheet as a clue. Avoid putting someone on the spot to read aloud or making the puzzle a competition unless everyone wants that.

For a conversation afterwards, ask which word they would add to a festive puzzle. Accept different traditions and the choice not to join in. This is a leisure activity; we do not claim it improves memory, treats stress or is suitable for every visual or cognitive need.
