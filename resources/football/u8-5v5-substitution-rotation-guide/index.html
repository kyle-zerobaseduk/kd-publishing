U8 5v5 Substitution Rotation: A Simple Match-Day Planning Guide

Plan U8 5v5 substitutions with checked six- and eight-player examples, goalkeeper notes and a free adaptable A4 rotation template.

Start with minutes, then choose roles

In ordinary 5v5, five players are on the pitch including the goalkeeper. Multiply the planned match minutes by five to find the player-minutes available, then divide by the squad attending. For a 40-minute example, there are 200 player-minutes. Six players average 33⅓ minutes; eight average 25. This arithmetic does not decide which positions children should play.

This page provides an optional planning method for U8 football in England. The worked examples assume a 40-minute fixture that can be organised into four 10-minute periods, permission for rolling substitutions and no additional-player arrangement. Your competition determines its actual playing periods and substitution requirements. Confirm them before filling in the sheet. This is not a U7 3v3 rotation guide.

The official position and our recommendation

The FA’s 2026/27 Mini-Soccer handbook allows players to return through roll-off, roll-on substitutions with the referee’s permission. It recommends equal playing time where possible, with at least 50% per player as best practice. That recommendation is not a claim that every competition has the same enforceable minimum. Check your competition’s rules, including any welfare exceptions.

Our recommendation is to plan a fair share before kick-off, explain it to children and families, and record what actually happened. Adapt for injury, distress, late arrival or a child’s choice. Do not keep a struggling child on just to satisfy a spreadsheet, and do not repeatedly give the same child the least time because the match is close.

Six players: a simple four-period example

Use A–F as placeholders for names. Every row has five playing children. This easy plan makes only one rest-slot change at each period break; it does not achieve exactly equal time in one match. A and B play 40 minutes; C, D, E and F play 30. Rotate the full-match roles at the next comparable fixture, recording totals across the group rather than promising that one plan settles fairness.

Eight players: an equal-time five-minute example

When permitted and practical, eight five-minute windows can give all eight children 25 minutes. Do not treat the clock times as automatic entry permission: make the change at a suitable stoppage with the referee’s agreement and record the actual minute. If frequent changes disrupt the group, use fewer planned changes and accept a small difference, then balance it over later games.

Goalkeeper time counts too

Include goalkeeper minutes in each child’s total. In the six-player example, A could keep goal for the first half and B for the second, with both playing outfield in their other periods. These are optional roles, agreed with willing children, not a requirement to swap keepers. Give everyone a chance to learn over time without putting an unwilling child under pressure.

In the eight-player example, D is on for the first two windows, A for the next two, B for the next two and C for the final two, so D / A / B / C can each take a 10-minute goalkeeper turn. Those roles are only a worked example. Check shirts and a calm handover at every swap. If one child keeps goal for all 40 minutes, the other seven share 160 outfield minutes, averaging about 22.9 each; the all-player equal-time table no longer applies unchanged.

Seven players or a different match length

For seven in a 40-minute 5v5 fixture, the average is about 28.6 minutes. Choose manageable windows, check that exactly five children play in each and total their minutes. A perfectly equal paper schedule is less useful than one that can be followed calmly. If the match is shorter, replace the example timings rather than copying the totals.

The template gives eight blank windows, a space for the goalkeeper, and a planned/actual total for each child. Use only as many rows as you need. Write rest names first, then confirm the five players on the pitch. Keep personal medical information off a sheet that other people might see.

Make substitutions calm and visible

Tell the child who is resting when you expect their next turn. Bring the next player to the agreed touchline area, get the referee’s permission, and make sure the outgoing child leaves before the incoming child joins. Avoid shouting several names while the ball is moving. One adult can track the sheet while the coach watches play, if the club has suitable support.

A child resting is still part of the team: keep them supervised and appropriately warm, with access to water. Give them a simple thing to notice rather than a running punishment. Do not ask substitutes to retrieve balls into traffic or behind another pitch.

Reconcile the actual minutes

After the game, add actual pitch time including goalkeeper spells. For standard 5v5 throughout, the combined total should be five times the match length: 200 minutes in this example. If a player was temporarily off, the fixture stopped early, or permitted powerplay changed the numbers, note that and recalculate. Record the reason for a difference without turning it into public criticism.

In the six-player table the totals are 40 + 40 + 30 + 30 + 30 + 30 = 200. In the eight-player table they are 8 × 25 = 200. The printed worked examples and the webpage use the same records and are checked programmatically. Actual substitutions may differ; the blank sheet is for adapting and recording, not enforcing an unchangeable plan.
