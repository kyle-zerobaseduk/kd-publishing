# Phase 2B resources architecture and organic content plan

Prepared 9 October 2026. **Planning only: no resource pages, downloads or posts are published by Phase 2A.** Ranking below is an editorial judgement about commercial fit, production effort and differentiation. It is not a search-volume or keyword-difficulty estimate. Search results are a snapshot, not a reproducible Google UK rank report. Appearance in a search establishes a competing page, not proven customer demand for K.D.Publishing.

## Focus and information architecture

Grassroots football is the strongest durable cluster: two complementary books, specific parent/volunteer problems and an opportunity for approved first-hand experience. Nostalgia puzzles offer a faster genuine-sample route. Workplace gifts offer a seasonal test with one relevant book; avoid presenting the publisher as an independent reviewer. Broad terms such as `football drills`, `free word search`, `Secret Santa gifts` and `journaling prompts` are too competitive to be the first targets.

Keep the approved header, footer, typography, cards, colours and responsive CSS. After approval of the first useful resources, add one Resources navigation link and these indexable hubs:

| Proposed route | Purpose | Initial condition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `/resources/` | Help visitors choose football, puzzles or workplace humour | Launch only with real approved resources |
| `/resources/football/` | Session plans and practical coaching sheets | Launch with at least two substantive resources |
| `/resources/puzzles/` | Genuine puzzle samples and print guidance | Launch with at least two substantive resources |
| `/resources/workplace-humour/` | Excerpts and transparent gift guidance | Launch when the two planned resources are approved |

The conditions above are our publishing standards, not Google minimums. Do not publish empty hubs. Book purchase pages keep their existing URLs and transactional purpose. Resources answer practical questions; they link naturally to relevant books rather than repeating product descriptions.

`scripts/resource_template.py` supplies a reusable body using existing `page-head`, `container`, `prose`, `section`, breadcrumb and book-card components. It has no publishing command and is not called by the site build. It requires an explicit approval flag, accountable author, review date, useful HTML sections and one or two related books; it escapes supplied text. It cannot establish truth or substitute for owner review. Future integration must add shell metadata, Article/BreadcrumbList markup, all-page link/sitemap checks, navigation and accessible PDF checks together. The current validators intentionally expect 35 pages and prohibit committed PDFs; Phase 2B must explicitly extend those constraints for approved resources rather than bypass them.

## Page contract and linking rules

Each resource needs one stable descriptive URL and one H1, a concise search title plus the existing brand suffix, a unique description explaining the actual task, self canonical, matching Open Graph URL and a byline/review date. Use actual publication/review dates, not automatically refreshed dates. Mark up Article and the visible breadcrumb trail; never claim credentials, reviews, FAQ-rich-result eligibility or official endorsement. Cite current primary guidance beside rule statements.

Include: a direct useful answer; equipment/players/time or puzzle specification; clear instructions; relevant variations and limitations; an optional approved printable; printing notes and answer key where applicable; one or two contextual book recommendations; sources and an error/contact route. Length follows the task, not a word quota. Put useful text, diagrams with explanatory alt text and puzzle word lists in HTML. A PDF alone is not the resource page.

HTML route `/resources/{cluster}/{slug}/`; downloadable derivatives under `assets/resources/{slug}.pdf`. PDF filenames stay stable, contain attribution, a source-page URL and a version/date. Publish limited approved excerpts, never full interiors or print masters. Confirm reproduction permission and check solutions, A4/Letter fit, font legibility and real printed output. Do not stretch raster previews and call them large-print PDFs. Prefer text-preserving accessible PDF exports. Explain whether printing at actual size or fit-to-page changes type size. Any personal-use or club-use licence requires owner approval.

Every page receives a crawlable link from its cluster hub; hubs link from Resources and appropriate category/book pages. Each resource links to one or two genuinely relevant books and at most a few related resources. Add reciprocal book links where helpful. Use descriptive anchors such as “U7 & U8 Season Planner”, not repetitive keyword strings. No UTM tags on internal or Amazon links. No links to unpublished routes. Do not split near-identical `first U7 session` and `first U8 session` articles: use one training page with age-specific match-format notes. Keep U7 carousel and U8 substitution advice separate because their official match formats differ.

## Football facts and approval

The founder manages a grassroots U8 team in England according to the owner. That is first-hand experience, not evidence of a coaching qualification. Before publication, have the owner approve the actual situation, attendance, activity, adjustments and observations. Record what happened without identifying children, including photos/names only with separately established permission. Do not invent anecdotes or measurable development outcomes.

Current primary sources checked: [FA FutureFit](https://futurefit.englandfootball.com/futurefit/index.html), [age formats and balls](https://futurefit.englandfootball.com/futurefit/faqs/has-the-ball-size-changed-for-different-age-groups/), and [England Football 5v5 guidance](https://learn.northeurope.englandfootball.com/articles-and-resources/coaching/resources/2025/How-to-play-and-manage-5v5-games). From 2026/27 the published pathway places U7 at 3v3 and U8 at up to 5v5, with size 3 balls. Training can use smaller games. Do not label a U7 substitution sheet as official 3v3 practice. The owner's specific league and its current operating rules have not been identified, so resource-level claims about match duration, supervision, safeguarding procedures or local competition must be checked against that league/club and current official rules before publication. No County FA is assumed from an old document or the founder's identity.

## £0 distribution after content approval

Keep existing marketing automation unchanged in this stage. For each approved resource, make two Pinterest variants (useful resource cover and actual sample), one Instagram carousel showing the problem/solution with a website link route, and one useful Facebook post. Adapt the wording rather than posting identical promotions everywhere. Direct readers to the HTML resource, with a natural book link inside it. Start with 1–2 useful resource-led posts per week across existing channels; revise frequency from engagement, not assumed algorithm rules.

For relevant football communities, follow their promotion rules and answer a real question first. Share the resource only when it directly helps; disclose that it links to our books. Use existing authorised club contacts only after approval of the exact message and recipients. No outreach, posts, bot changes or backlink requests have been made. Cite helpful official resources in content; do not buy links, exchange keyword links, fabricate testimonials or solicit artificial visits.

Use inbound campaign tags only: `utm_source=pinterest|instagram|facebook`, `utm_medium=organic_social`, `utm_campaign=resources_football_2026q4` (or `resources_puzzles_2026q4`, `resources_humour_2026q4`), `utm_content=small_squad_card_01` or another stable asset ID. Some GA4 reporting rules classify `social` differently from `organic_social`; verify the intended channel grouping without changing account settings here. Keep a simple campaign register of destination, publish date and asset ID. Search traffic remains untagged. Review actual resource visits and related-book interest, not impressions reported by social automation alone.
